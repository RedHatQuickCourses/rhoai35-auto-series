= Governing AI Data Workflows: AutoML and AutoRAG Strategies
:navtitle: Home

== Welcome

Across the industry, organizations are learning a hard truth: serving an AI model is getting easier, but getting an AI data pipeline reliably into production is still remarkably difficult. More than 75% of enterprise AI data initiatives stall out, trapped inside exploratory Jupyter notebooks that work on a developer's laptop but never survive the transition to a governed, scalable production endpoint. 
 
This training course is designed to solve that specific bottleneck. Rather than focusing on platform administration or YAML cluster setup, this course places you directly in the practitioner's seat (Data Scientists, AI Engineers, and Technical Solution Architects). 
 
You will explore how Red Hat OpenShift AI’s automated capabilities: AutoML, AutoRAG, and MLflow Governance; take raw enterprise data sitting in object storage and systematically convert it into production-ready, auditable inference APIs in hours rather than months.

== Goal

Teach technical professionals how to strategically evaluate use cases and apply AutoML (predictive modeling) and AutoRAG (generative retrieval) in Red Hat OpenShift AI 3.5+ to drive measurable business value by showcasing its targeted automated AI capabilities.

NOTE: In OpenShift AI 3.5, AutoML and AutoRAG are *Technology Preview* features. Both are targeted to reach *General Availability* in 3.6. This course teaches the 3.5 workflow and flags what changes at GA as we go.

== Objectives

On completing this course, you should be able to:

* Predictive Foundations (AutoML): Evaluate when to apply AutoML to convert raw tabular data or time series into production-ready predictive models across classification, regression, and time-series tasks.

* AutoML governance & deployment: Use AutoML evaluation artifacts (leaderboards, feature importance, ROC/PR curves) to validate business logic and promote winning models to production.

* GenAI prompting & MLflow governance: Transition from predictive data to generative text, leveraging the GenAI Playground and MLflow to version, track, and eliminate "prompt drift".

* Generative retrieval (AutoRAG): Position AutoRAG as an automated optimization engine that benchmarks chunking, embedding, and retrieval configurations (search mode, top-k, and more) to build grounded LLM applications.

* Knowledge review and assessment: Track core enablement concepts across AutoML, MLflow prompt tracking, and AutoRAG to validate practitioner capability in diagnosing enterprise data scenarios and selecting optimal AI patterns.

== Audience

* *Primary Personas:* Data Scientists, AI Engineers, and Technical Solution Architects evaluating or adopting Red Hat OpenShift AI 3.5+.

* *Technical Level & Focus (TL2):* Technical practitioners and field specialists looking to understand the "why", decision points, trade-offs, and business outcomes of automated capabilities. Focused on transitioning from manual ML/RAG development without needing to perform cluster admin setup procedures.

* *Distribution & Accessibility:* Publicly discoverable enablement designed for global Red Hat field teams, ecosystem partners, and enterprise customers.

== Prerequisites

This course assumes that you have the following experience:

* Familiarity with core machine learning concepts (classification, regression, time series).

* Familiarity with generative AI concepts (LLMs, prompting, retrieval-augmented generation).

* Familiarity with Red Hat OpenShift AI 3.5+ from a practitioner (Data Scientist / AI Engineer) perspective.

Notice that no cluster administration or infrastructure setup experience is assumed or required.

== Classroom environment

This course requires no hands-on user labs. It uses diagrams, Summit Arcade interactive clickthroughs, and video walkthroughs to demonstrate AutoML and AutoRAG capabilities, and it closes with a scenario-based graded knowledge check.
