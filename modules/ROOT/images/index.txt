Welcome to this course on transforming raw data into working models using AutoML and AutoRAG on OpenShift AI.

For many of us, the actual process of managing data can be daunting. There is a lot to consider—from cleaning and structuring to backing it up and shaping it for machine learning. If you have ever felt that you would rather focus on getting business results than becoming a data-cleaning specialist, then this course is designed exactly for you.

There is a hard truth in the AI industry: more than 75% of enterprise AI initiatives stall, often trapped in exploratory notebooks that work on a developer's laptop but never make it to a scalable, production-ready endpoint.

In this course, we place you directly in the practitioner's seat. We will skip the infrastructure plumbing and complex cluster setup. Instead, you will see how Red Hat OpenShift AI’s "Auto" capabilities—including AutoML, AutoRAG, and prompt management—can take raw enterprise data and turn it into auditable, production-ready inference APIs in a matter of hours, not months.

It is important to keep the bigger picture in view. AutoML and AutoRAG are designed to remove the manual grind from the development process, without requiring you to be an ML specialist who must hand-tune algorithms or build pipelines from scratch. For many business problems, the results produced by these tools are more than sufficient for production. Reaching a high-quality result quickly allows your team to focus their energy on higher-value deliverables. And, if a problem does demand deeper customization, these fast results help you determine early on whether your data is worth that additional investment.

Remember that Red Hat OpenShift AI is the central integration point. It connects your data to everything else you need, including inference serving, hardware profiles, workbenches, and the model registry. While AutoML and AutoRAG get you to a working model fast, the platform is what allows you to put that model to work.

Our goal for this course is to teach you how to evaluate use cases and apply predictive modeling with AutoML and generative retrieval with AutoRAG in OpenShift AI version 3.5 and above. Please note that in version 3.5, these are technology preview features, which are targeted to reach general availability in version 3.6. We will focus on the 3.5 workflow and highlight what changes as we move to general availability.

By the end of this course, you will be able to perform several key tasks. First, you will learn to evaluate when to apply AutoML to convert raw data into predictive models for classification, regression, or time-series tasks. Second, you will learn to use built-in evaluation reports—such as leaderboards and feature importance curves—to validate results against your business logic. Third, you will learn to transition to generative text, using the GenAI Playground to manage prompts and prevent prompt drift. Fourth, you will see how AutoRAG automatically tunes document searching and chunking to provide your LLM with the most accurate, grounded answers. And finally, you will learn to track these core concepts to diagnose enterprise data scenarios effectively.

This course is intended for data scientists, AI engineers, and technical architects who are ready to adopt or evaluate Red Hat OpenShift AI. We assume you have a working knowledge of core machine learning and generative AI concepts, and some familiarity with the OpenShift AI practitioner interface. You do not need to be an expert, and you certainly do not need to worry about cluster administration or infrastructure setup.

Because this is a conceptual and practical overview, there are no hands-on user labs. Instead, we use interactive click-throughs and video walkthroughs to demonstrate these capabilities using real-world examples. As a tip, watch these closely to understand why specific choices are made, so you can learn to interpret the results yourself.

For further reading, you can always consult the official OpenShift AI product documentation or visit the Red Hat AI Examples repositories on GitHub.

Finally, we want to highlight that this content was created with the assistance of AI tools, though all materials have been authored by humans and reviewed by domain experts. Let’s get started.
