== AutoGluon

Under the covers, RHOAI AutoML is powered by *AutoGluon*, which acts as a meta-orchestrator. Instead of asking you to bet on one algorithm and tune it by hand, AutoGluon runs *parallel sweeps* across several model families at once, then figures out which ones actually deserved their place. 

Think of an algorithm as a mathematical recipe, and a model family (or software library) as the specific engine built to execute that recipe. What AutoGluon does as a "meta-orchestrator" is manage a whole workshop full of different engines at the same time, rather than making you guess which one will work best.

Those candidates come from two broad families.

== "Smart flowchart builders" (gradient-boosted decision trees)

These models learn by chaining together lots of yes/no splits, like a flowchart that keeps correcting its own mistakes. They're the tried and true of tabular prediction:

* *XGBoost*, the meticulous veteran. Uses exact, depth-wise tree splits. A dependable default for standard spreadsheet and financial data.
* *LightGBM*, the speed demon. Built by Microsoft, it uses fast histogram-based, leaf-wise splitting to chew through multi-gigabyte datasets with low memory overhead.
* *CatBoost*, the category specialist. Built by Yandex, it handles categorical text columns natively, so you don't have to manually encode them.

== "Synthetic brains" (deep neural networks)

* *Tabular MLPs (PyTorch / FastAI)*, multi-layer perceptrons that capture complex, non-linear interactions between your columns, patterns the tree models can miss.

== The real trick: stacked ensembling

Here's the part that matters most. AutoGluon doesn't just crown a single winning algorithm and walk away. It combines the predictions of the top candidates into a multi-layer *Weighted Ensemble*, letting the models cover for each other's blind spots. That stacked approach consistently beats hand-tuning any single model by roughly *2–5% in accuracy*.

TIP: First time seeing these names? Don't worry about choosing between XGBoost, LightGBM, and the rest. Just know that when a "WeightedEnsemble" model tops your leaderboard, it isn't a bug or a mystery entry: it's usually the blended model, and it's often your best performer.
