Under the Hood: How AutoML Builds Its Models

Estimated reading time: 8 minutes.

Objective

Explain what AutoGluon is doing during a run and why the leaderboard is an ensemble, not a single algorithm.

You don’t strictly need to know what happens between "start run" and "here’s your leaderboard" to use AutoML. But a little bit of under-the-hood knowledge pays off the moment you start reading results, because the names you’ll see on the leaderboard suddenly mean something.

AutoGluon, the conductor

Under the covers, RHOAI AutoML is powered by AutoGluon, which acts as a meta-orchestrator. Instead of asking you to bet on one algorithm and tune it by hand, AutoGluon runs parallel sweeps across several model families at once, then figures out which ones actually earned their place. Think of it less as a single musician and more as a conductor auditioning a whole orchestra.

Those candidates come from two broad families.

"Smart flowchart builders" (gradient-boosted decision trees)

These models learn by chaining together lots of yes/no splits, like a flowchart that keeps correcting its own mistakes. They’re the bread and butter of tabular prediction:

  • XGBoost, the meticulous veteran. Uses exact, depth-wise tree splits. A dependable default for standard spreadsheet and financial data.

  • LightGBM, the speed demon. Built by Microsoft, it uses fast histogram-based, leaf-wise splitting to chew through multi-gigabyte datasets with low memory overhead.

  • CatBoost, the category specialist. Built by Yandex, it handles categorical text columns natively, so you don’t have to manually one-hot encode them.

"Synthetic brains" (deep neural networks)

  • Tabular MLPs (PyTorch / FastAI), multi-layer perceptrons that capture complex, non-linear interactions between your columns, patterns the tree models can miss.

The real trick: stacked ensembling

Here’s the part that matters most. AutoGluon doesn’t just crown a single winning algorithm and walk away. It combines the predictions of the top candidates into a multi-layer Weighted Ensemble, letting the models cover for each other’s blind spots. That stacked approach consistently beats hand-tuning any single model by roughly 2–5% in accuracy.

First time seeing these names? Don’t worry about choosing between XGBoost, LightGBM, and the rest, letting you skip that decision is the whole point of AutoML. Just know that when a "WeightedEnsemble" model tops your leaderboard, it isn’t a bug or a mystery entry: it’s usually the blended model, and it’s often your best performer.
