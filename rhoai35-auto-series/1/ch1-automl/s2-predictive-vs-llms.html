Where Predictive AI Fits (and Where It Doesn’t)

Objective

Position predictive models against LLMs on cost and behavior, and qualify whether a dataset is a good fit for AutoML.

Walk into almost any room in 2026 and the conversation is about large language models. Which is great, LLMs are genuinely transformational. But there’s a trap hiding in all that excitement: reaching for a giant GPU-hungry model to answer a question that a tiny, boring, deterministic model would answer better, cheaper, and a thousand times faster.

Before examining specific use cases, let me outline the exact domains where predictive AI proves most effective.

Predictive models vs. LLMs

The easiest way to see it is side by side. Same job description, two very different employees:

Dimension Predictive Models (AutoML / Tabular) Large Language Models (LLMs)

Primary scope

Narrow, single-task structured decisions (churn, fraud, pricing)

Broad, open-domain reasoning and text generation

Input structure

Low-dimensional structured tabular data (10–100+ features)

High-dimensional unstructured text across billions of tokens

Artifact size

Kilobytes to megabytes (lightweight trees / ensembles)

Tens to hundreds of gigabytes (billions of parameters)

Hardware

Lightweight CPU infrastructure, no specialized GPUs

High-VRAM GPU clusters

Latency

Microsecond inference for high-throughput systems

Second-level response times

Auditability

Deterministic logic with clear feature importance

Probabilistic reasoning needing guardrail evaluation

The takeaway: predictive models are cheap, fast, and deterministic. They scrape millions of rows on the CPU clusters you already own, at a fraction of the cost of a GPU fleet, and they give you the same answer every time with a clear paper trail for why. When the question is "will this transaction be fraud?" you don’t need a model that can also write poetry. You need one that’s right, fast, and explainable.

Is your data even a candidate?

AutoML is powerful, but it’s not magic, it expects a certain shape of data. Before you kick off a run, check that your dataset qualifies:

  • Tabular structure. Rows are entities, columns are attributes, stored as CSV. Files should be UTF-8, comma-delimited, with a header row. You can upload up to 32 MiB through the dashboard UI, or ingest up to 100 MB directly from S3.

  • A clear target label. One column holds the ground-truth answer you want to predict for new data.

  • Feature diversity. A healthy mix of numeric and categorical columns, that’s where AutoML’s automatic type inference, missing-value imputation, and encoding earn their keep.

  • Enough volume. At least 500–1,000 rows to prototype, and 10,000+ is typical for a serious enterprise evaluation.

When AutoML is not the tool

Just as important is knowing when to put it down and reach for something else:

  • Unstructured media, images, audio, video. Those need specialized deep-learning pipelines.

  • Open-ended text generation, chatbots, document synthesis, summarization. That’s the world of LLMs and AutoRAG, which we’ll get to later in the course.

  • Custom domain edge cases, problems that hinge on hand-crafted business rules a model can’t infer from the raw table.

Match the tool to the shape of the problem and AutoML feels like a superpower. Force it onto the wrong problem and it just frustrates everyone. Now that you can spot a good fit, let’s look at the four flavors of prediction it can actually do.
