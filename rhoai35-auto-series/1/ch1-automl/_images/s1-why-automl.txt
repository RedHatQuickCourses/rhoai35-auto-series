So why Auto M L? Let's start with the business case, because if you can't explain the value to the people signing the checks, even the most brilliant model never ships.

Here's the problem we're solving: traditional data science is slow, manual work. A data scientist does something heroic but repetitive, cleaning messy data, engineering features, testing one algorithm after another, tuning parameters to claw back half a percent of accuracy. It's skilled, careful work. It's also the same grind on every project.

That grind is exactly what Auto M L automates. Instead of a person hand-running each step, the platform takes over the heavy lifting: it preps your data by inferring column types and filling in missing values, engineers features to pull out meaningful signals, tests many algorithm families in parallel, and then ensembles the best candidates into a model stronger than any single one.

So the split is simple. You bring the judgment, the business question, and the data. Auto M L handles the repetitive technical work in the middle.

A useful way to picture it is as a Sorting Specialist. You hand it raw tabular data, say a CSV pulled from S3-compatible object storage, and it finds the patterns hiding inside. What comes back is a ranked leaderboard of candidate models, sorted best to worst, ready for you to review and promote. You're no longer building models one at a time and hoping; you're reviewing a graded shortlist.
