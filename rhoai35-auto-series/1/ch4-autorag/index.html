Generative Retrieval with AutoRAG

Goal

Position AutoRAG as an automated optimization engine that benchmarks chunking, embedding, and vector database configurations for grounded LLM applications.

At the end of chapter 3 we hit an honest limit: a beautifully governed prompt still only knows what the model already learned during training. Ask it about your company’s return policy, last quarter’s field procedures, or the fine print in a specific contract, and it will either shrug or, worse, confidently make something up.

The standard fix is RAG, Retrieval-Augmented Generation: before the model answers, you retrieve the relevant chunks from your own documents and hand them over as context. It works. The catch is that building RAG well is a grind. How big should the chunks be? Recursive or structural chunking? Vector search or hybrid? Which embedding model? How many chunks to retrieve? Every combination is a knob, and tuning them by hand is weeks of trial and error, exactly the kind of manual benchmarking you learned to avoid back in chapter 1.

AutoRAG is AutoML’s idea applied to retrieval. Instead of hand-tuning, you give it your documents and a set of test questions, and it automatically searches across chunking, embedding, and retrieval configurations, scores each one against your questions, and hands back a ranked leaderboard of production-ready RAG patterns. If chapter 1’s AutoML was a Sorting Hat for models, AutoRAG is a Sorting Hat for retrieval pipelines.

AutoRAG is a Technology Preview feature in OpenShift AI 3.5. It’s the platform’s rung-3 answer, context grounding, right after prompt governance and before you’d ever consider fine-tuning a model.

The demo below walks the whole thing end to end for a fictitious bank. It’s worth watching once now for the shape of it; we’ll unpack each stage in the pages that follow.
