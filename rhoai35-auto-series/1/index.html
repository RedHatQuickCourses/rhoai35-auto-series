From Raw Data to a Working Model with AutoML and AutoRAG on OpenShift AI

Data Without the Dread

Data is the part a lot of us quietly avoid. There is so much to hold in your head: how to structure it, how to clean it, how to back it up, how to shape it for machine learning. If you have ever thought "I don’t want to become a data-cleaning specialist, I just want to use this data to get the results my business needs," you are exactly who this course is for.

That instinct is backed by a hard industry truth. More than 75% of enterprise AI data initiatives stall out, trapped inside exploratory notebooks that run fine on a developer’s laptop but never survive the trip to a governed, scalable production endpoint.

This course puts you in the practitioner’s seat, not the platform administrator’s. No YAML cluster setup, no infrastructure plumbing. Instead, you’ll see how Red Hat OpenShift AI’s "Auto" capabilities (AutoML, AutoRAG, and prompt management) take raw enterprise data sitting in object storage and turn it into production-ready, auditable inference APIs in hours rather than months.

Keep the bigger picture in view

AutoML, AutoRAG, and prompt management are not the whole story, and they are not trying to be. What they do is remove the manual grind between raw data and a working model, without asking you to be an ML specialist who hand-tunes algorithms or hand-builds pipelines. For plenty of real business problems, the model they produce is good enough to put into production as-is. Not every solution needs the highest accuracy possible, and reaching a good-enough result in hours frees your team to spend its energy on the harder, higher-value deliverables. When a problem does demand more, that same fast result tells you early whether the data is even worth the deeper investment.

The platform carries a different kind of weight. Red Hat OpenShift AI is the integration point that connects this data to everything else you will build on top of it: inference serving, GPUs and hardware profiles, workbenches, the model registry, and the data connections that feed real applications. AutoML and AutoRAG get you to a working model fast. The platform is what you use to actually put it to work.

Goal

Teach technical professionals how to evaluate use cases and apply AutoML (predictive modeling) and AutoRAG (generative retrieval) in Red Hat OpenShift AI 3.5+ to drive measurable business value.

In OpenShift AI 3.5, AutoML and AutoRAG are technology preview features. Both are targeted to reach general availability in 3.6. This course teaches the 3.5 workflow and flags what changes at GA.

Objectives

On completing this course, you should be able to:

  • Evaluate when to apply AutoML to convert raw tabular data or time series into production-ready predictive models across classification, regression, and time-series tasks.

  • Use AutoML’s built-in evaluation reports, the scorecards that tell you which model won and why, to check the results against your business logic before you promote a model to production. (leaderboards, feature importance, ROC/PR curves)

  • Transition from predictive data to generative text, leveraging the GenAI Playground and prompt management to version, track, and eliminate "prompt drift".

  • Let AutoRAG do the tuning for you. It automatically tests different ways of slicing and searching your documents to find the setup that gives an LLM the most grounded, accurate answers. (chunking, embedding, search mode, top-k, and more)

  • Track core enablement concepts across AutoML, prompt tracking, and AutoRAG to validate practitioner capability in diagnosing enterprise data scenarios and selecting optimal AI patterns.

Audience

  • Primary Personas: Data Scientists, AI Engineers, and Technical Solution Architects evaluating or adopting Red Hat OpenShift AI 3.5+.

  • Technical Level & Focus (TL2): Technical practitioners and field specialists looking to understand the "why", decision points, trade-offs, and business outcomes of automated ML/RAG capabilities. Focused on transitioning from manual ML/RAG development without needing to perform cluster admin setup procedures.

  • Distribution & Accessibility: Publicly discoverable enablement designed for global Red Hat field teams, ecosystem partners, and enterprise customers.

What you bring to the table

You do not need to be an expert in any of this. You just need to be comfortable enough to follow along. It helps if you have:

  • A working sense of core machine learning ideas (classification, regression, time series). You don’t need to have built them, just to recognize them.

  • A working sense of generative AI ideas (LLMs, prompting, retrieval-augmented generation).

  • Some exposure to Red Hat OpenShift AI 3.5+ as a practitioner, not as an administrator.

And here is what you can leave at the door: no cluster administration, no infrastructure setup, no data-engineering pipeline building. That is the whole point.
