Throughout this course, you have gained three powerful capabilities: AutoML for predictive modeling, AutoRAG for grounded generation, and prompt management for governing your models. Individually, these tools solve specific problems. Together, they create a comprehensive business process. To see how they integrate, let’s follow a single decision from raw data all the way to a final human action.

### The End-to-End Workflow: A Loan Desk Scenario

Imagine a loan officer reviewing an application. To make an informed decision, they need to answer two distinct questions.

First, they need to know the risk associated with the applicant. This is a classic use case for AutoML. You feed your historical loan data into the system, which evaluates models like XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, and neural networks to produce a ranked leaderboard. Once you promote the winning model to a serving runtime on KServe, you can retrieve a precise risk score from a REST API in microseconds.

Second, the officer needs to understand the "why" behind that score and the corresponding policy requirements. A raw number isn’t enough for compliance. They need a formal credit memo—for example, "This applicant is in the elevated-risk band; per section 4 of our policy, applications above $250,000 require a secondary review." This is where AutoRAG comes in. It retrieves the relevant policy chunks and drafts a memo grounded in your company's actual documents, rather than relying on what the model might have memorized during training.

Supporting both processes is prompt management. The system instructions that dictate the tone and requirements of the credit memo are versioned and stored in a registry backed by MLflow. This provides a clear audit trail, allowing you to prove exactly which prompt version generated a specific memo during compliance reviews.

Essentially, the platform handles the tedious heavy lifting—predictive AI for the numbers and generative AI for the language—so the human officer can focus their expertise where it matters most: making the final judgment.

### Diagnostic Decision Logic

Building this workflow is one skill, but knowing how to troubleshoot it is what separates a user from an operator. When results look off, diagnose each stage separately.

For your AutoML model, rely on your report card. Never trust raw accuracy on imbalanced data; instead, examine your confusion matrix and ROC-PR curves. Also, check feature importance to ensure the model isn't relying on irrelevant or biased data.

For AutoRAG, use the metric triad we discussed previously. Don't look at scores in isolation; look for patterns. For instance, high faithfulness combined with low correctness tells you the model is answering honestly based on its context, but it was provided the wrong information. In this case, you should fix your retrieval or chunking strategy, not the model itself. Conversely, high correctness paired with low faithfulness suggests the model got lucky by relying on its own memory rather than your documents—a dangerous trend that won't hold up with unseen data.

For prompt management, your diagnostic tool is the audit trail. When an answer is incorrect, check the lineage in the registry to see which version was used, compare it against previous versions, and roll back if necessary.

Always identify the failing stage before you start changing settings. A low score is just a symptom; the pattern is the diagnosis.

### When "Auto" Is Enough

It is vital to know when these automated capabilities are the right tool for the job. Stick with these automated solutions when your problem involves predictable, structured data; when your GenAI app simply needs access to your specific facts; or when the base model already possesses the core skill—like summarization—but lacks your internal knowledge.

Consider escalating beyond automation only when you encounter a skill gap that cannot be solved by better prompting or retrieval. For example, if a model consistently fails to maintain a specific, required tone or output format regardless of the context provided, that may be a sign that you need to move toward fine-tuning a specialized model. Keep in mind that fine-tuning is a heavier, more expensive process, and you should only reach for it once you have exhausted all other options.
